## Problem Statement

The current FundOps screener is a traditional filter system dressed up with an AI wizard. You set 80 preset filters (min gross margin = 30%, max P/E = 25, etc.), the AI fills in values faster than you can drag sliders, and the screener runs a fixed scoring formula. It's a fancy form.

The real problem: every investor has a different definition of "cheap," "quality," and "good." A SaaS investor and a bank investor use completely different metrics. And the screener has no memory. It doesn't know what you liked last time, what worked, or what failed. Every run starts from zero.

Thomas built a version of the right system for himself in FundOps: sector-aware metrics, backward DCF as a universal second check, Claude Code writing the scoring function. But it was a personal tool, not a product. The challenge is making that approach work for any investor, at any skill level, with a learning loop that gets smarter over time.

## The Vision in One Sentence

The AI IS the screener. You have a conversation to define your strategy. The AI generates the scoring logic. It learns from your feedback and market outcomes. It gets better at finding what YOU want.

## Core Design Principles

### 1. The conversation IS the product
Most investors can't articulate their strategy precisely until someone asks them the right questions. The strategy definition conversation, where the AI probes, challenges, and crystallizes your investment philosophy, is the most valuable part of the experience. The filters are just the output.

### 2. Three autonomy modes
Not everyone wants the same level of AI involvement:
- **Copilot**: AI recommends, you decide everything. Full human control.
- **Advisor**: AI runs the pipeline automatically (screen, thesis, IC review). You only decide buy/sell.
- **Autopilot**: AI manages the full loop including position sizing. You set constraints and review monthly.
Users start in Copilot, graduate as they build trust. The architecture supports all three from day one.

### 3. Two learning signals, human dominates
- **User feedback** (immediate, subjective): thumbs up/down, promote to thesis, dismiss with reason
- **Market outcomes** (delayed, objective): did fundamentals hold? Did the stock outperform?
The AI accumulates both signals but NEVER changes your strategy without approval. It proposes improvements in a "Strategy Review" space. You accept, reject, or modify.

### 4. Learning applies to ALL agents
The feedback loop isn't just for the screener. Thesis, IC Review, Memo, Allocator all improve over time through the same mechanism. "Your best theses focused on unit economics." "Your IC Reviews missed customer concentration risk 3 times."

### 5. Human-readable everything
AI-generated code uses internal keys like `pct_cashflow`. Every key gets an auto-generated human-readable label, unit, and formatting hint. The user never sees snake_case.

## Ten Functions

### Function 1: Strategy Definition
**What it does**: Office-hours-style conversation where the AI helps you articulate your investment strategy.

**How it works**:
- Open-ended start: you describe what you're looking for in natural language
- AI listens, extracts what it can, then probes gaps
- "You mentioned quality and cheap, but what does 'cheap' mean to you specifically? Backward DCF? P/E below sector median? Earnings yield above cost of equity?"
- Offers concrete options via buttons (not just open text) so users who don't know finance terms can still participate
- Extracts 7 dimensions:
  1. North star goal (1 sentence)
  2. What "cheap" means (backward DCF, P/E, earnings yield, sector-relative)
  3. What "quality" means (margins, ROIC, cash conversion, consistency)
  4. Sector preferences (include/exclude, different metrics per sector)
  5. Risk tolerance (max leverage, require profitability, allow pre-revenue)
  6. Time horizon (1yr trade or 5yr hold)
  7. Position sizing context (concentrated 10-15 or diversified 50+)
- First-time: deep conversation (5-10 exchanges)
- Subsequent: "Your strategy is X. Want to adjust anything before I screen?"

**Output**: Strategy Profile (stored as JSON + natural language description)

**Autonomy mode impact**:
- Copilot: Full conversation, you approve every dimension
- Advisor: Same initial setup, then runs on its own. Strategy Review proposes changes.
- Autopilot: Same initial setup + constraint setting (max position size, sector limits, risk budget)

### Function 2: Universe Selection
**What it does**: Determines which stocks to screen.

**How it works**:
- AI recommends the best universe based on your strategy
- Can use web search to find constituents: "Let me look up the Russell 3000 members"
- Proposes: "For your 'undervalued compounders' strategy, I'd screen all US stocks above $500M market cap, weighted toward mid-cap $2-10B where mispricings are more common"
- You approve, modify, or override with a preset/custom list
- Saved as part of your strategy profile

**Data sources**:
- Preset lists (S&P 500, Nasdaq 100, US Large Cap 200, etc.) bundled with FundOps
- Custom ticker lists (paste or CSV)
- AI-generated via web search (dynamic, needs approval)

### Function 3: Data Collection
**What it does**: Pulls raw financial data for the universe.

**Architecture**:
- **Prices**: yfinance (free, not configurable)
- **Fundamentals**: SEC EDGAR (free, raw XBRL, computes everything from scratch)
- **Estimates**: FMP (optional paid, analyst consensus only)
- No provider switching UI. SEC is the answer for fundamentals.

**Two-phase approach**:
1. Quick filter via yfinance (prices, market cap, sector). ~30s for 500 tickers.
2. SEC deep enrichment on survivors. ~5-8min first run, cached 12-24hr after.

### Function 4: Metric Computation
**What it does**: Computes all financial metrics from raw SEC XBRL data.

**Already built** in `backend/core/sec/`:
- Margins (gross, operating, net, EBITDA, FCF)
- Returns (ROE, ROA, ROIC, ROCE)
- Growth (revenue, EPS, FCF, 1Y/3Y/5Y CAGR, consistency)
- Leverage (D/E, net debt/EBITDA, interest coverage)
- Cash flow quality (FCF conversion, income quality, capex intensity)
- Owner earnings (Buffett-style)
- Quality scores (Piotroski F-Score, Altman Z-Score)
- Backward DCF (implied growth rate priced in by market)
- Sector-specific KPIs (bank NIM, tech Rule of 40, REIT FFO, etc.)
- Sector medians for relative comparison

**New for v2**: The AI can request custom computed metrics not in the standard set. E.g., "operating leverage = this year operating margin minus last year." The metric computation layer needs to accept arbitrary formulas from the scoring function.

### Function 5: Scoring Logic (AI-Generated Python)
**What it does**: The AI generates a Python scoring function based on your strategy conversation.

**How it works**:
1. Strategy conversation (Function 1) produces a Strategy Profile
2. The AI generates a Python function: `def score(stock: dict) -> dict`
3. The function uses metrics from Function 4
4. It implements sector-aware routing: different logic for banks vs tech vs industrials
5. It returns a score + breakdown explaining WHY each stock ranked where it did
6. The generated code is saved, versioned, and inspectable
7. On subsequent runs, the same code is reused unless the strategy changes

**Example generated code**:
```python
def score(stock):
    sector = stock.get('subsector', '')

    # Cheapness: sector-aware
    if sector == 'banking':
        cheapness = ptbv_score(stock)  # Price to tangible book
    elif sector == 'tech':
        cheapness = ev_fcf_score(stock)  # EV/FCF
    else:
        cheapness = backward_dcf_gap(stock)  # Implied vs actual growth

    # Quality: sector-aware
    if sector == 'banking':
        quality = nim_efficiency_score(stock)
    else:
        quality = roic_margin_score(stock)

    # Combine
    total = cheapness * 0.4 + quality * 0.35 + growth_durability(stock) * 0.25

    return {
        'score': total,
        'cheapness': cheapness,
        'quality': quality,
        'growth': growth_durability(stock),
        'reason': f"{'Cheap' if cheapness > 7 else 'Fair'} {sector} with {'strong' if quality > 7 else 'moderate'} fundamentals"
    }
```

**Display label map** (auto-generated alongside the code):

**Guardrails**:
- Sandboxed execution (no file I/O, no network, no imports beyond math/statistics)
- Function must return within 100ms per stock
- Scoring code reviewed by the user before first execution (Copilot mode)
- Version history: every change to scoring logic is logged with timestamp and reason

### Function 6: Result Presentation
**What it does**: Shows screener results with dynamic columns based on what the AI scored.

**Layout**:
- Fixed columns: Ticker, Company, Sector, Price, Score
- Dynamic columns: pulled from the AI's scoring function return keys. If it scored on `cheapness`, `quality`, `growth`, those appear as columns.
- Human-readable labels (from the label map): `cheapness` shows as "Cheapness", `ptbv` shows as "P/Tangible Book 1.2x"
- Click any row to expand AI reasoning panel: "PAYC scored 8.2 because: Cheapness 7.5 (backward DCF gap 9.2%, market prices in 3% growth vs actual 12%), Quality 8.8 (ROIC 34%, gross margin 83%, FCF conversion 110%)"
- The AI also outputs a per-stock "reason" string in natural language

**Ask during strategy conversation**: "What metrics do you want to see in the results table?" User picks the columns they care about most.

### Function 7: Feedback Capture
**What it does**: Records your reactions to screener results.

**Signals (strongest to weakest)**:
1. **Bought the stock**: strongest positive (comes from portfolio import/manual add)
2. **Promoted to Thesis**: strong positive (clicked "Generate Thesis")
3. **Thumbs up**: moderate positive
4. **Thumbs down with reason**: strong negative with learning context
5. **Thumbs down without reason**: moderate negative
6. **Ignored**: weak negative (might just mean didn't look)

**Dismiss reasons** (structured, not free text):
- Too much debt
- Cyclical/commodity business
- Management concerns
- Already own enough in this sector
- Too small / too large
- Don't understand the business
- Valuation already reflects quality
- Custom reason (free text)

**Storage**: Every screener result row gets a feedback column. Stored in DB with timestamp, strategy version, and scoring logic version.

### Function 8: Outcome Tracking
**What it does**: Grades screener results against market reality, independent of user feedback.

**Two tracks**:

**Fundamentals track** (30/60/90 day check):
- Pull updated SEC financials for screened stocks
- Compare to screening-time values: did margins hold? Did growth continue? Did debt increase?
- Score: "Thesis Integrity" 0-100. 100 = everything the screener liked about this stock is still true.

**Performance track** (3/6/12 month check):
- Price return since screened date
- Compare: top-ranked stocks vs bottom-ranked vs benchmark (S&P 500)
- Score: "Screener Alpha" = average return of top 10 minus S&P 500 return over same period

**Goal-relative grading (the real measure)**:

Price return is a SECONDARY signal. The PRIMARY measure is: did the screener correctly identify stocks that match the user's north star?

If the north star is "undervalued compounders with durable moats," grading looks like:

| Dimension | What to check | Timeframe |
|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| "Compounder" | Did revenue grow consistently? Did margins hold or expand? Did ROIC stay above threshold? | Quarterly (each earnings) |
| "Undervalued" | Did the backward DCF gap close? Did the market re-rate closer to fair value? | 6-12 months |
| "Durable moat" | Did competitive position hold? No margin compression from new entrants? Market share stable? | 12+ months |

A stock that went up 50% on a meme squeeze is NOT a screener success. It found the wrong thing for the right reason. A stock that went down 10% but maintained 25% ROIC, expanded margins, and got CHEAPER (wider DCF gap) is a success. The screener found the right company. The market hasn't caught up yet.

The learning loop measures: **did the screener correctly identify stocks that match the north star, regardless of short-term price?**

This means:
- Each strategy profile defines what "success" looks like beyond returns
- The AI generates goal-specific outcome checks from the north star conversation
- "Undervalued compounders" gets checked on compounding + valuation gap closure
- "Dividend growers" gets checked on dividend growth + payout sustainability
- "Sector dislocation plays" gets checked on whether the dislocation closed (re-rating)

**Returns matter, but the PATH to returns is the goal.**

Everyone wants returns. The question is: did the stock deliver returns THROUGH your chosen risk path, for the reasons you expected?

- PAYC +30% because margins expanded and DCF gap closed → SUCCESS (returns through the right path)
- PAYC +30% because buyout rumor leaked → NOT a screener success (right stock, wrong reason, got lucky)
- PAYC -10% but margins expanded and ROIC held → PARTIAL success (right identification, market timing off, thesis intact)
- PAYC -30% because margins collapsed → FAILURE (wrong identification, quality wasn't durable)

The learning loop weights both: goal alignment (did the thesis hold?) AND returns (did you make money?). But it distinguishes between returns earned through your strategy's intended risk path vs returns from noise. This prevents the system from drifting toward momentum chasing while still caring about the outcome that matters: making money the way you intended.

**Correlation analysis**:
- Which scoring dimensions predicted goal-aligned outcomes? (Not price, but thesis integrity)
- Which sectors did the screener identify correctly vs incorrectly?
- Which strategy versions produced the most goal-aligned results?

**Storage**: Outcome snapshots stored in DB, linked to screener run ID, strategy version, and goal-specific success criteria.

### Function 9: Learning Loop
**What it does**: Combines user feedback + market outcomes to propose strategy improvements.

**How it works**:
1. After N screener runs (or monthly, whichever comes first), the AI reviews accumulated data
2. It analyzes patterns:
   - "You consistently promote high-ROIC companies (>20%) and dismiss ones with D/E > 1.5"
   - "Stocks with backward DCF gap > 8% outperformed those with gap 5-8% by 2x"
   - "Your scoring penalizes banks heavily but the 2 bank stocks that passed went up 30%"
3. It generates a "Strategy Review" report with specific suggestions
4. Each suggestion is actionable: "Raise backward DCF gap threshold from 5% to 8%" or "Add bank-specific screening with P/TBV < 1.5"

**What it NEVER does**:
- Changes your strategy without approval
- Auto-adjusts scoring weights
- Removes a filter you set
- Overrides your judgment

**What it CAN do in Advisor/Autopilot modes**:
- In Advisor mode: auto-runs the pipeline but presents strategy changes for approval
- In Autopilot mode: can adjust scoring weights within user-defined bounds. E.g., "You said cheapness weight can range 30-50%. I'm moving it from 40% to 45% based on recent performance." Logged and reviewable.

### Function 10: Collaboration
**What it does**: The ongoing conversation between you and the AI about your investment approach.

**Where it lives**: "Strategy Review" tab/conversation in the app.

**Periodic review** (monthly or after N runs):
- AI prepares a report: what was screened, what you did with results, what outcomes showed
- Surfaces contradictions: "You say you want compounders but you keep promoting cyclical value trades"
- Proposes adjustments with evidence
- You discuss, accept/reject/modify

**Always-available chat**:
- "Why did PAYC rank higher than VEEV this time?"
- "What if I added a minimum 3-year revenue growth requirement?"
- "Show me how my scoring changed over the last 3 months"

**Cross-agent learning**:
- "Your Thesis agent's best reports focused on unit economics. Should the screener weight FCF metrics higher?"
- "IC Review flagged customer concentration risk 3 times in the last month. Should the screener check for customer concentration?"
- This applies to ALL agents, not just the screener.

## Autonomy Mode Architecture

| Function | Copilot | Advisor | Autopilot |
|----------|---------|---------|-----------|
| Strategy Definition | Full conversation | Same initial setup | Same + constraints |
| Universe Selection | AI recommends, you approve | AI picks, you can override | AI manages |
| Data Collection | Manual trigger ("Run Screener") | Auto-scheduled (weekly) | Auto-scheduled |
| Scoring Logic | AI generates, you review code | AI generates, auto-applies | AI generates + auto-adjusts within bounds |
| Result Presentation | You review every result | Top picks surfaced, rest available | Summary only |
| Feedback Capture | Thumbs up/down on every result | Only on promoted/dismissed | Implicit from position changes |
| Outcome Tracking | Shown in Strategy Review | Auto-runs, feeds learning | Auto-runs, feeds auto-adjustment |
| Learning Loop | Proposals in Strategy Review | Same, with auto-pipeline | Can auto-adjust within bounds |
| Collaboration | On-demand + periodic review | Periodic review + alerts | Monthly review + constraint check |

## Cross-Model Perspective

Independent cold read by Claude subagent (did not see the brainstorming conversation):

**Steelman**: "The first screener that gets better the more you use it, and the first where two users with the same data see completely different results because the scoring logic itself is personalized."

**Key insight**: "The scoring code generation quality IS the product. The prompt engineering for Function 5 is the hardest and most valuable engineering problem in the entire design."

**Challenged premise**: User feedback may be nearly useless for improving screening quality. Investors' thumbs-up/down reflects biases (name recognition, sector mood), not forward-looking insight. Evidence test: after 20+ runs, if user feedback suggestions contradict outcome-based suggestions more often than they agree, user feedback is noise.

**Resolution**: Premise kept but refined. User feedback and market outcomes are TWO SEPARATE MODELS, never blended. Taste model (user feedback) = what you want to see. Performance model (outcomes) = what actually works. Both surfaced independently. Conflicting signals are the user's decision. Taste-training never pollutes the performance model.

**48-hour prototype**: Build ONLY Function 5 (scoring code generation) + Function 6 (results table). Hardcode a strategy profile. Test one hypothesis: can the LLM write a sector-aware scoring function that produces rankings an experienced investor agrees with? If that doesn't work, nothing else matters.

## Open Questions

1. **Code sandboxing**: How do we safely execute AI-generated Python scoring functions? RestrictedPython? Subprocess with timeout? Docker container?
2. **Autopilot regulations**: Does FundOps need to register as an investment advisor if it makes autonomous portfolio decisions? Probably yes in some jurisdictions.
3. **Outcome tracking frequency**: How often do we check fundamentals? Every earnings report (quarterly) makes sense. Price can be checked daily.
4. **Cross-agent learning data model**: How do learnings from IC Review flow back to Screener? Shared memory? Event system?
5. **Multi-strategy support**: Can a user have multiple strategies running simultaneously? E.g., "Value" and "Growth" strategies screening different universes.

## Success Criteria

1. A user can describe their investment strategy in natural language and get a screening run that matches their intent within 3 conversation exchanges
2. The scoring logic uses sector-appropriate metrics automatically (no manual routing)
3. After 10 screener runs with feedback, the AI's "Strategy Review" surfaces at least one insight the user hadn't explicitly stated
4. Outcome tracking shows whether top-ranked stocks actually outperform bottom-ranked within 90 days
5. The system supports all three autonomy modes with clean mode switching
