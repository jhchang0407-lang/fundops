Core thesis: Check Point is a cash-generating cybersecurity compounder trading at a significant discount to high-growth peers. The investment case rests on three complementary return sources that don't depend on each other to work.

1. Discount Closing (+15%): CHKP trades at 18x forward P/E versus the cybersecurity sector median of 28x. This discount reflects legacy perceptions of CHKP as a "slow grower" — but the subscription transition (now 68% of revenue, up from 52% two years ago) is reshaping the revenue profile. As subscription mix crosses 70-75%, we expect the market to re-rate CHKP closer to SaaS-like multiples. Even a partial close to 22x P/E implies 15%+ upside from valuation alone.

2. Subscription Growth (+11%): Subscription revenue is growing 11.3% with improving visibility. The Infinity architecture is driving platform consolidation within the existing customer base (150,000+ customers), and the transition from perpetual licenses to subscriptions creates a natural tailwind as customers migrate. SEC filings confirm the acceleration: subscription ARR grew from $1.8B to $2.1B in the latest fiscal year.

3. Capital Return (+5%): CHKP has $3.1B in cash with zero debt and generates $1.44B in annual FCF. The company is returning $1.5B+/yr via buybacks, reducing share count approximately 5% annually. This is a structural compounder — even if the stock doesn't move, you're getting 5% annual return from shrinking the denominator.

Why it's cheap: The market still prices CHKP as an on-prem firewall vendor despite the accelerating cloud/subscription transition. Analysts model conservative 8-9% growth, but the subscription segment is growing at 15%+ and becoming a larger share of the mix. The re-rating catalyst is mathematical — it happens as subscription crosses threshold percentages.

Key assumptions to monitor: (1) Subscription growth stays above 11%, (2) Operating margins hold above 40%, (3) Buyback pace sustains above $1.5B/yr, (4) Subscription mix continues increasing, (5) No significant competitive displacement from CrowdStrike or Palo Alto in core network security.
